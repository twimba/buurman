This agreement is governed by the Act on Residential Leases (laki asuinhuoneiston vuokrauksesta, 481/1995, hereinafter AHVL). The provisions of the Act may be departed from by agreement only where the Act does not provide otherwise and the departure is not otherwise to be regarded as prohibited (AHVL section 3). This document is drafted under the legislation of mainland Finland. A dwelling located in the Åland Islands is governed by the province’s own tenancy legislation (Hyreslag för landskapet Åland, 1999:19), under which this document has not been drafted.

The tenancy starts on start date.

The lease is concluded for an indefinite period (toistaiseksi voimassa oleva).

The lease is concluded for a fixed term (määräaikainen) and ends on end date. A fixed-term lease ends when its term expires (AHVL section 4, subsection 2). A fixed-term lease ends by notice of termination only in the cases provided by law (AHVL section 51, subsection 2).

If a tenancy with the same tenant is agreed more than 2 times in succession for a fixed term of at most 3 months, the lease is in force for an indefinite period notwithstanding the term on the fixed period (AHVL section 4, subsection 1).

If the tenant, at least 3 months before the fixed term ends, notifies the landlord that the tenant wishes to continue the tenancy and the landlord refuses without a reason regarded as acceptable in a tenancy, the tenant is entitled to compensation for the repair and alteration works carried out by the tenant that have increased the value of the dwelling, at their value at the end of the tenancy, if the tenant was entitled by law to carry out the works and they have not already been compensated (AHVL section 58).

The lease and any amendment to it are made in writing. If the lease has not been made in writing, it is deemed to be in force for an indefinite period (AHVL section 5, subsection 1). An electronic agreement in a form that can generally be stored and reproduced for a period appropriate to its purpose also counts as written (AHVL section 5 a).

During the tenancy, the landlord or the tenant may bring the reasonableness of the rent or of a term on its determination before a court (AHVL section 29, subsection 1). The landlord may not give notice of termination while the reasonableness of the rent is being examined by the district court (käräjäoikeus) at the tenant’s request (AHVL section 29, subsection 2).

This document is not drafted for dwellings whose rent is subject to the rent-setting provisions of the ARAVA restrictions legislation or the interest-subsidy legislation (AHVL section 27, subsection 4).

The landlord must notify the tenant in writing of the increase, the new rent and the date on which the increase takes effect. The increased rent takes effect at the earliest 1 month after the start of the rent payment period next following the notice, and it may not be charged retroactively. At the tenant’s request, the landlord must provide, free of charge and without delay, information on the basis of the increase and on how the new rent is calculated (AHVL section 27, subsection 2).

The security covers all obligations arising from the tenancy unless otherwise agreed. If the security is not provided within the agreed time, the landlord is entitled to rescind the agreement, except where the security has been provided before the notice of rescission was received (AHVL section 8, subsection 1).

A term under which the security exceeds an amount equal to 3 months’ rent is void (AHVL section 8, subsection 5). The tenant is therefore not obliged to provide security above that amount.

The security must be returned or released, or a written notice of its retention must be given, without delay and no later than within 14 days after the lease ended or after possession was surrendered later; if there is an impediment for a justified reason, the measure must be taken without delay and no later than within 14 days after the impediment ceased. The notice must state the grounds of the claim covered by the security and an estimate of its amount made in a reasonably required manner (AHVL section 8, subsection 2).

The part of the security retained may not exceed the amount of the claim or the notified estimate of its amount; however, security deposited in a rent deposit account may be retained in full until the amount of the claim has been established (AHVL section 8, subsection 3). The person who provided the security is entitled to compensation for damage caused by an unjustified retention or failure to release, unless the landlord shows that it was not caused by a reason, omission or other negligence attributable to the landlord (AHVL section 8, subsection 4). The dwelling may be used for residential purposes only. The tenant may use the dwelling as the tenant’s own home or as a joint home with the tenant’s spouse and the children belonging to the family (AHVL section 17, subsection 1).

If the tenant uses the dwelling together with other persons, the tenant must notify the landlord in writing of the number of residents and the basis of their right to live in the dwelling, and of the names, dates of birth and contact details of the adult residents. If that notice is not given when the lease is concluded, it must be given without delay after the tenant has obtained possession of the dwelling and thereafter whenever a new resident moves in; the moving out of a notified resident must also be notified in writing without delay (AHVL section 17 a).

The tenant must take good care of the dwelling (AHVL section 25, subsection 1), and the tenant must comply with the house rules (järjestyssäännöt) of the housing company.

Provided that this does not cause the landlord considerable inconvenience or disturbance, the tenant may also use the dwelling as a joint home with a close relative of the tenant or of the tenant’s spouse, or may sublet or otherwise give up to half of the dwelling for use by another person as a home (AHVL section 17, subsection 1). In other cases, giving the dwelling or part of it for use by another person requires consent in the lease or permission given separately by the landlord (AHVL section 17, subsection 2).

The tenant may give the entire dwelling for use by another person as a home for at most 2 years if the tenant is temporarily staying in another locality because of work, studies, illness or another comparable reason, or if there is another special reason for doing so, and the landlord has no justified reason to object. The tenant must notify the landlord in writing, no later than 1 month before the transfer of use, of the transfer, its duration and the person to whom the dwelling is given; a landlord who does not accept the transfer must, within 14 days of receiving the notice, bring the grounds of the objection before a court (AHVL section 18).

Unless otherwise agreed, the tenant remains liable for the tenant’s obligations and for damage that the person who received the dwelling for use is obliged to compensate to the landlord (AHVL section 19). The tenant may not assign the tenancy without permission given in the lease or separately by the landlord, unless the law provides otherwise, as for an assignment of the tenancy to a family member (AHVL section 44, subsection 1, section 45).

The tenant must notify the landlord without delay of damage to the dwelling or of a defect for whose repair the landlord is responsible, and immediately if the repair must be carried out without delay to prevent the damage from increasing (AHVL section 24, subsection 1). The tenant is not liable for ordinary wear and tear if the landlord is responsible for the condition and maintenance of the dwelling, but compensates damage that the tenant, or a person staying in the dwelling with the tenant’s permission, causes to the dwelling intentionally, by omission or by other negligence (AHVL section 25).

The tenant is entitled to be released from paying rent or to a reasonable reduction of the rent for the time during which the dwelling could not be used or was not in the required or agreed condition, except where the defective condition results from the tenant’s omission or other negligence or the repair or alteration work was carried out because of damage for which the tenant is liable; there is no such right for the time before the landlord was informed of the defect (AHVL section 23, subsection 2).

The tenant has no right, without the landlord’s permission, to carry out repair or alteration works in the dwelling other than to remedy a defect that the landlord has failed to repair, but the tenant may always take measures to prevent or limit immediate damage (AHVL section 21, subsection 1). The landlord may carry out immediately work that cannot be postponed without causing damage, and work that causes no substantial inconvenience after notifying the tenant at least 14 days before the work starts (AHVL section 21, subsection 2). For other repair or alteration works the landlord must give notice at least 6 months before the work starts, and the tenant is entitled, within 14 days of receiving the notice, to rescind the agreement with effect from the earliest date on which the work may begin (AHVL section 21, subsection 3).

The landlord and the landlord’s representative are entitled to enter the dwelling when this is necessary to supervise its condition and upkeep or for repair and alteration works or upkeep measures, and to show the dwelling if it is to be sold or is available for re-letting; the visit is arranged at a time suitable for the parties unless the urgency or nature of the matter prevents this (AHVL section 22). A term restricting the tenant’s statutory rights concerning the condition of the dwelling, or making the tenant’s liability stricter than provided by law, is void (AHVL section 26).

The landlord has made a valid energy performance certificate (energiatodistus) for the building or part of it available to the tenant at the viewing and gives it to the tenant as an original or a copy (Act on Energy Performance Certificates for Buildings, laki rakennuksen energiatodistuksesta, 50/2013, section 6, subsection 1).

The energy performance certificate need not be made available or given if the letting is for a fixed term of at most 1 year, if it is a sublease or a letting between entities of the same group (Act 50/2013, section 6, subsection 2), or if the building is excluded from the scope of that Act (Act 50/2013, section 3).

The landlord must keep the dwelling available to the tenant on the day on which the tenant is entitled to obtain possession of it (AHVL section 16, subsection 1). The parties inspect the dwelling together before it is handed over and record its condition, the keys handed over and the meter readings in an inspection report, which each party signs and of which each party receives a copy.

The moving day (muuttopäivä) is the day on which the lease ends (AHVL section 68). The tenant returns the dwelling and all keys to the landlord no later than on that day, and the parties inspect the dwelling together. In a tenancy for an indefinite period, a court may postpone the moving day under the conditions provided by law (AHVL section 69).

Property left in the dwelling after the tenancy has ended passes to the landlord without consideration under the conditions and time limits provided by law (AHVL section 68 a). The return of the security is governed by this agreement (see article ).

The notice period (irtisanomisaika) of a lease for an indefinite period starts to run from the last day of the calendar month during which notice was given (AHVL section 52, subsection 1). The landlord’s notice period is 4 months if the tenancy has lasted continuously for at least 2 years immediately before the notice, and otherwise 3 months (AHVL section 52, subsection 2). The tenant’s notice period is 1 month (AHVL section 52, subsection 3).

A term that shortens the landlord’s notice period or lengthens the tenant’s notice period is void, as is a term under which the notice period may start for the first time only after a certain period from the start of the tenancy (AHVL section 52, subsection 4).

The landlord’s notice of termination must be given in writing and must state the date on which the tenancy ends and the ground for the termination (AHVL section 54, subsection 1). The tenant’s notice of termination is given in writing to the landlord or to the person whom the landlord has instructed to collect the rent, but not to a financial institution (AHVL section 54, subsection 2). The notice of termination must be served in a verifiable manner (todisteellisesti) (AHVL section 13 a, subsection 1), and a termination made in any other way is ineffective (AHVL section 54, subsections 5 and 6).

Where spouses live in the dwelling as their joint home, a spouse may not terminate the lease without the other spouse’s consent, unless the consent cannot be obtained without difficulty (AHVL section 53, subsection 1).

At the tenant’s request, the court must declare a termination made by the landlord ineffective in the cases provided by law (AHVL section 56, subsection 1). The claim must be brought while the tenancy lasts and no later than within 3 months after the notice of termination was received (AHVL section 56, subsection 2).

A fixed-term lease (see article ) cannot be terminated by notice other than in the cases provided by law; on a special ground provided by law, a court may authorise the tenant or the landlord to terminate the lease by notice (AHVL section 55, subsection 1).

The landlord is entitled to rescind (purkaa) the lease on the grounds provided by law, such as where the tenant fails to pay the rent within the statutory or agreed time (AHVL section 61, subsection 1); there is no such right if the conduct giving rise to the ground for rescission is of minor significance (AHVL section 61, subsection 3). On certain grounds, rescission requires that the tenant has first been given a written warning (AHVL section 62). The tenant is entitled to rescind the lease on the grounds provided by law, such as where using the dwelling for the purpose presupposed in the agreement causes an obvious danger to the health of the tenant or of a member of the tenant’s household (AHVL section 63, subsection 1). The notice of rescission must be given in writing and must state the ground for rescission (AHVL section 66, subsection 1).

The parties may always agree on the end of the tenancy. The return of the dwelling is governed by this agreement (see article ).

The parties seek to resolve disagreements primarily through negotiation. A tenant who is a consumer, or a landlord who is a private individual, may bring a disagreement relating to the lease of a dwelling before the Consumer Disputes Board (kuluttajariitalautakunta), which issues a recommendation on the matter (Act on the Consumer Disputes Board, 8/2007, section 2, subsection 1, paragraph 4).

Disputes are decided by the competent district court (käräjäoikeus) in accordance with the venue provisions of Chapter 10 of the Code of Judicial Procedure (oikeudenkäymiskaari). A civil dispute based on the tenancy of a dwelling may be heard in the small-claims procedure as provided in Chapter 6 a of the Code of Judicial Procedure.
