This agreement creates a tenancy of a dwelling to which the Residential Tenancies Act 2004, as amended (hereinafter “the Act”), applies, unless the dwelling is excluded by section 3(2) of the Act, for example a dwelling within which the landlord also resides, a dwelling let to a person whose entitlement to occupation is for the purpose of a holiday only, or a dwelling used wholly or partly for the purpose of carrying on a business such that the occupier could, after the tenancy has lasted 5 years, make an application under section 13(1)(a) of the Landlord and Tenant (Amendment) Act 1980. In this agreement “the Board” means the Residential Tenancies Board (RTB).

This agreement is drafted for a tenancy created on or after 1 March 2026. For a tenancy created before that date different transitional rules of the Act apply, in particular on the setting of the rent and on the grounds on which the landlord may terminate the tenancy.

Where a provision of the Act that cannot be excluded by agreement conflicts with this agreement, the provision of the Act prevails. A term that is inconsistent with the Act is not a term of a Part 4 tenancy (section 30(3) of the Act), and rights of the tenant that are more beneficial than those created by Part 4 of the Act are not affected (section 26).

The tenancy starts on start date.

The tenancy is a periodic tenancy and continues from rent period to rent period until it is terminated in accordance with Part 5 of the Act.

The tenancy is granted for a fixed term ending on end date. The expiry of the fixed term does not by itself end a Part 4 tenancy: the landlord may terminate a Part 4 tenancy only in accordance with section 34 of the Act (section 33).

Where the tenant has been in occupation of the dwelling under the tenancy for a continuous period of 6 months and no notice of termination giving the required period of notice has been served before the expiry of that period (or any such notice has been withdrawn), the tenancy continues in existence as a Part 4 tenancy for a period of unlimited duration from the date on which that period of 6 months expires, subject to termination in accordance with the Act (section 28).

Where the tenancy commences on or after 1 March 2026, the Part 4 tenancy is a relevant Part 4 tenancy. Each continuous period of 6 years under it, from the commencement of the relevant Part 4 tenancy and from each sixth anniversary of that commencement, is a tenancy of minimum duration (section 35B(1) of the Act), during which the grounds on which the landlord may terminate the tenancy are further restricted. The termination of the tenancy is governed by what this agreement provides (see article ).

The rent is amount, payable monthly. Utilities and other charges are dealt with separately (see article ).

The rent set under this tenancy may not be greater than the market rent for the tenancy at the time it is set (section 19(1) of the Act). In addition, section 19(4) of the Act limits the rent: the new rent may not exceed the old rent by more than 2 per cent of the old rent for each year that has elapsed since the previous setting of the rent (and a proportionate percentage for any shorter additional period), nor in a greater proportion than the All Items Consumer Price Index (CPI) published by the Central Statistics Office has increased between the previous setting and the new setting, whichever limit is lower. For a new tenancy of the dwelling the old rent is the rent last set under the tenancy that immediately preceded it within the meaning of section 19(4)(c) of the Act. The limit of 2 per cent does not apply, but the CPI limit does, to dwellings in an apartment complex or in student accommodation for whose construction, extension or change of use a commencement notice or a 7 day notice was given on or after 10 June 2025, subject to the conditions of section 19(4)(aa) of the Act.

Section 19(5) of the Act lists the cases in which these limits do not apply to the rent first set under this tenancy, for example (subject to the conditions of that subsection) where no tenancy of the dwelling subsisted during the 2 years immediately preceding the creation of this tenancy, or where the most recent previous tenancy of the dwelling was terminated by the tenant.

The rent may be reviewed only in accordance with Part 3 of the Act. For a tenancy that commences on or after 1 March 2026, a review of the rent may not occur more frequently than once in each period of 12 months, nor in the period of 12 months beginning on the commencement of the tenancy (sections 20(1) and 20B(2) of the Act), unless, within that period, a substantial change within the meaning of section 19(5A) in the nature of the accommodation occurs and the rent, were it to be set immediately after that change, would by virtue of that change be different to the market rent at the time of the last review or of the commencement of the tenancy (section 20(3)).

The new rent may not be greater than the market rent and must comply with the limits of section 19(4) of the Act, except in the cases listed in section 19(5), including the rent set under the tenancy following a period of not less than 6 years during which section 19(4) applied to the rent and every setting of the rent complied with it (section 19(5)(aa)).

A new rent does not have effect unless, at least 90 days before the date from which it is to have effect, the landlord serves on the tenant a notice in the prescribed form stating the amount of the new rent, the date from which it is to have effect and the other matters required by section 22(2A) of the Act, including the landlord’s statement that in the landlord’s opinion the new rent is not greater than the market rent, the rent information in the published register of the Board for 3 dwellings of a similar size, number of bedrooms, type, character and (where applicable) BER in a comparable area, the floor area of the dwelling and (where applicable) its BER, and a copy of that notice is served on the Board within 7 days of the date on which it is served on the tenant (section 22(2) of the Act).

A dispute about the new rent must be referred to the Board before the date from which the new rent is to have effect or before the expiry of 28 days from the receipt of the notice by the tenant, whichever is the later (section 22(2A)(b) of the Act).

The condition of a refusal or failure is satisfied if, after all reasonable attempts, the landlord or the authorised agent could not be contacted to make the request (section 12(5)).

The tenant notifies the landlord or the authorised agent of any defect that arises in the dwelling and requires repair, allows reasonable access for works for which the landlord is responsible, and does not do any act that would cause a deterioration in the condition of the dwelling beyond normal wear and tear (section 16(d), (e) and (f) of the Act). The tenancy may be terminated by the landlord or the tenant only by a notice of termination served in accordance with Part 5 of the Act, and not by a notice of forfeiture, a re-entry or any other process not provided by that Part (section 58(1) of the Act). A notice of termination is valid only if it is in writing, is signed by the landlord or the authorised agent or, as appropriate, by the tenant, specifies the date of its service, is in the prescribed form (if any), states the reason for the termination where the Act requires it, specifies the termination date (the day, month and year on which the tenancy terminates and on or before which, for a termination by the landlord, the tenant must vacate, the tenant having the whole of the 24 hours of that date to vacate) and states that any issue as to its validity or the right to serve it must be referred to the Board within the period referred to in section 80 (section 62(1)). A notice of termination served by the landlord is invalid unless a copy of it is served on the Board within 7 days of the date on which it is served on the tenant (section 62(1A)).

Termination by the tenant. The tenant may terminate the tenancy by a notice of termination giving at least the period of notice of Table 2 to section 66 of the Act, according to the duration of the tenancy: less than 6 months, 28 days; 6 months or more but less than 1 year, 35 days; 1 year or more but less than 2 years, 42 days; 2 years or more but less than 4 years, 56 days; 4 years or more but less than 8 years, 84 days; 8 years or more, 112 days. Where the landlord fails to comply with the obligations of the tenancy, the tenant may terminate it by a notice giving 7 days’ notice if the landlord’s behaviour poses an imminent danger of death or serious injury or an imminent danger to the fabric of the dwelling or the property containing it, and otherwise 28 days’ notice, provided that the tenant has notified the landlord in writing of the failure and the landlord has not remedied it within a reasonable time (section 68). During a fixed term the tenant may terminate the tenancy before its end only in those cases, under section 186 (refusal of consent to an assignment or sub-letting), or by agreement with the landlord.

Termination by the landlord. During a fixed term the landlord terminates the tenancy only for the tenant’s failure to comply with the obligations of the tenancy, in accordance with the Act. Unless the termination is for such a failure, the landlord’s notice of termination gives at least the period of notice of Table 1 to section 66 of the Act, according to the duration of the tenancy: less than 6 months, 90 days; 6 months or more but less than 1 year, 152 days; 1 year or more but less than 7 years, 180 days; 7 years or more but less than 8 years, 196 days; 8 years or more, 224 days. Before the tenancy has become a Part 4 tenancy, the landlord need not rely on a ground in the Table to section 34 of the Act, but the notice must state which of the reasons listed in section 62(1)(da) applies.

A Part 4 tenancy may be terminated by the landlord only on any of the grounds in the Table to section 34 of the Act, cited in the notice of termination, which must contain or be accompanied by the statement, statutory declaration or certificate that the Act requires for the ground concerned (sections 34 and 35): (1) the tenant has failed to comply with an obligation of the tenancy (other than the obligations in section 16(a) of the Act, such as the payment of rent), has been notified in writing of the failure by a notification stating that the landlord is entitled to terminate the tenancy if the failure is not remedied within a reasonable time specified in it, and has not remedied the failure within that time, unless the failure provides an excepted basis for termination; (1A) the tenant has failed to pay rent due, the landlord has notified the tenant and the Board in writing of the amount due, and that amount has not been paid within 28 days following receipt of the notification by the tenant or by the Board, whichever occurs later (section 67(3)); (2) the dwelling is no longer suitable to the accommodation needs of the tenant and of the persons residing with the tenant, having regard to the number of bed spaces and the size and composition of the household; (3) the landlord intends, within 9 months after the termination, to enter into an enforceable agreement to transfer, for full consideration, the whole of the landlord’s interest in the dwelling or in the property containing it; (4) the landlord requires the dwelling or the property containing it for the landlord’s own occupation or for occupation by a member of the landlord’s family; (5) the landlord intends to substantially refurbish or renovate the dwelling or the property containing it in a way which requires the dwelling to be vacated for that purpose, planning permission has been obtained where it is required, and a registered construction professional certifies that the works would pose a risk to the health or safety of the occupants and should not proceed while the dwelling is occupied, for a period specified in the certificate of not less than 3 weeks; (6) the landlord intends to change the use of the dwelling or of the property containing it to some other use, and planning permission has been obtained where it is required. On grounds (4), (5) and (6) the landlord must offer the tenancy back to the tenant in the circumstances set out in the Table. Section 35A of the Act further restricts ground (3), subject to the exceptions in that section, where the landlord intends to transfer 10 or more dwellings in the same development, each the subject of a tenancy, within a period of 6 months.

For a Part 4 tenancy that commences on or after 1 March 2026, section 35B of the Act applies: a landlord that is a company, or that is a landlord under more than 3 tenancies of dwellings on the date the notice is served, may not terminate the tenancy on grounds (3), (4), (5) or (6). Any other landlord may not terminate it on grounds (3), (5) or (6), except (a) on any of those grounds, where the notice of termination, giving the required period of notice, is served before the expiry of a tenancy of minimum duration and that period of notice expires on or after that expiry, or (b) on ground (3), where the landlord shows to the satisfaction of the Board that the landlord requires the consideration from the transfer to provide a principal private residence for the landlord or the landlord’s spouse or civil partner, or to discharge a debt or make a payment to the Revenue Commissioners of at least 15 per cent of the expected consideration that is legally required to be discharged or paid within 9 months after the termination date, or that the landlord or the landlord’s spouse or civil partner is in a personal insolvency, bankruptcy or composition or arrangement with creditors as described in section 35B(4), and that the restriction would, having regard to all the circumstances of the case, cause undue financial or other hardship on the landlord. Where the termination date falls before the expiry of a tenancy of minimum duration, ground (4) is available only for the landlord’s spouse, civil partner, child, stepchild, foster child, parent, step parent, parent-in-law or a person adopted by the landlord (section 35(4)(b)).

Where the termination is for the tenant’s failure to comply with the obligations of a Part 4 tenancy, the period of notice is 28 days, regardless of the duration of the tenancy, and 7 days where the tenancy is terminated by reason of anti-social behaviour within paragraph (a) or (b) of the definition in section 17(1) of the Act or of behaviour threatening the fabric of the dwelling or of the property containing it (section 67(2)).

A shorter period of notice than the Act requires may be agreed only at, or after, the time the other party indicates the intention to terminate; a term of this agreement cannot constitute such an agreement (section 69 of the Act).
