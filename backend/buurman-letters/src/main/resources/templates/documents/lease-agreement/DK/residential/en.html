Between

landlord, hereinafter “the landlord” (udlejer), and

tenant, hereinafter (jointly) “the tenant” (lejer),

the tenant(s) signing below, hereinafter (jointly) “the tenant” (lejer),

the following lease of a residential dwelling (lejeaftale om en beboelseslejlighed) is concluded. If there are several tenants, they are jointly and severally liable (solidarisk) for all obligations under this agreement.

The landlord lets to the tenant, for residential purposes, the dwelling at address with the rooms and appurtenances described in this agreement, hereinafter “the leased premises” (det lejede).

The tenancy is governed by the Danish Rent Act (lov om leje, lejeloven). This agreement is deemed concluded on the terms of the Act, to the extent that nothing else is expressly provided (§ 11, subsection 2). Provisions of this agreement that conflict with mandatory rules of the Act have no effect.

This agreement is not the authorised standard form (typeformular A, 10. udgave) under Executive Order no. 77 of 29 January 2025 on authorised standard forms for leases (bekendtgørelse om autoriserede standardblanketter for lejeaftaler). When a lease of a residential dwelling is concluded, standard forms (blanketter) may be used only if they are authorised (§ 14, subsection 1). Provisions of this agreement that impose greater obligations on the tenant or give the tenant fewer rights than provided in the Act are invalid (§ 14, subsection 3).

The tenancy starts on start date.

The tenancy runs until it is terminated by notice from either party under the rules of the Act.

The tenancy is for a fixed term (tidsbestemt) and ends without notice on end date. A fixed-term tenancy ends without notice when the agreed term expires and cannot be terminated by notice during the term, unless this has been agreed or the other party is in breach of the agreement (§ 173, subsection 1).

The housing court (boligretten) may set the time limit aside if it cannot be regarded as sufficiently justified by the landlord’s circumstances (§ 173, subsection 3).

Disagreements about the amount of the rent are decided by the rent tribunal (huslejenævnet).

For tenancies with cost-based rent in a regulated municipality (reguleret kommune, § 6, subsection 1), § 53 does not apply; the rent is instead adjusted under the rules of Chapter 3. The landlord’s right to a rent increase on account of increased property taxes and charges under §§ 46-48 and to an increase for improvements under the rules of the Act is not affected by this provision. For tenancies not covered by § 6, subsection 1, the following applies: a demand for a rent increase under § 42 must be made in writing, stating the amount of and the reason for the increase and informing the tenant of the right to object, and may be implemented with 3 months’ notice (§ 44, subsections 1 and 2).

If the landlord supplies heating and hot water, the landlord may claim reimbursement of the costs of the tenant’s consumption and of the tenant’s share of other costs under § 72, subsections 4 and 5; these costs cannot be included in the rent (§ 65, subsection 1). If the landlord supplies cooling, cold water, or electricity and gas, the landlord may claim reimbursement of the costs of the tenant’s consumption (§ 67, subsection 1, § 68, subsection 1, § 69). Reimbursed costs are allocated according to customary allocation rules based on consumption meters; if the property is exempt from the requirement of individual metering of cooling or cold water, those costs are included in the rent.

The tenant pays on-account contributions towards the tenant’s share of the costs, in equal amounts together with the rent. The total contributions for 1 year may not exceed the cost expected to be charged for the accounting period (§ 71, subsections 2 and 3).

The consumption statement (forbrugsregnskab) must have reached the tenant no later than 4 months after the end of the accounting year or, if the statement includes costs of supplies from a collective supply plant or a natural gas plant, no later than 3 months after the landlord has received the final settlement, if that is later (§ 75); if the statement has not arrived within that time limit, the landlord loses the right to claim an additional payment (§ 79, subsection 1). The tenant may object in writing no later than 6 weeks after the statement has arrived (§ 77). If an additional payment exceeds 3 months’ rent, the tenant may pay it in 3 equal monthly instalments (§ 78, subsection 1). Payment for a communal antenna and access to electronic communication services is made under § 84.

The deposit may not exceed an amount equal to 3 months’ rent (§ 59, subsection 1); an agreement on a higher deposit cannot be enforced for the part that exceeds 3 months’ rent (§ 64). The landlord may also require prepaid rent (forudbetalt leje) for a period of up to 3 months, provided that the prepaid rent does not at the same time exceed the rent payable in the period from the tenant’s notice of termination until the tenancy ends (§ 59, subsection 3). If a rent increase is implemented, an adjustment of the deposit and the prepaid rent may be required; if the rent is reduced, a corresponding part is repaid (§ 60, subsection 1). The deposit is repaid after the tenant has moved out, less the tenant’s documented obligations towards the landlord. Disagreements about the deposit are decided by the rent tribunal (§ 63, § 190). The move-out inspection is governed by what this agreement provides (see article ).

The tenant pays the rent (see article ) and the on-account contributions per month in advance, no later than day 1 of the month to which the payment relatesno later than day 1 of the month to which the payment relates, to the account with a bank in Denmark designated by the landlord (§ 57). Rent cannot be required to be paid for more than 3 months at a time (§ 58, subsection 3).

Payment is in time if made no later than on the due date. If the rent falls due on a public holiday, a Saturday or Constitution Day (grundlovsdag), the due date is postponed to the following weekday (§ 58, subsection 4).

In the event of late payment the tenant owes interest under § 5 of the Interest Act (renteloven). The landlord’s payment demand (påkrav) and the fee for it are described in the provision on the end of the tenancy.

The leased premises may be used only as a residence for the tenant and the tenant’s household. The tenant may not use the leased premises for any purpose other than the agreed one without the landlord’s consent (§ 150, subsection 1).

The tenant must treat the leased premises with due care and is liable for damage caused by careless conduct of the tenant, the tenant’s household or others whom the tenant has given access to the leased premises (§ 149, subsections 1 and 2). The tenant must comply with the general house rules (ordensregler) that apply to the property (§ 152, subsection 2).

The tenant may not, without the landlord’s consent, let persons other than members of the tenant’s household use the leased premises or any part of them, except where the Act gives the tenant a right to sublet (fremleje) (§ 150, subsection 2).

The tenant has the right to sublet up to half of the dwelling’s living rooms (beboelsesrum) for residential use; the total number of persons living in the dwelling may not exceed the number of living rooms (§ 157, subsection 1).

The tenant has the right to sublet a dwelling let exclusively for residential use for up to 2 years when the tenant’s absence is temporary and due to illness, a business trip, a period of study, a temporary transfer or similar (§ 158, subsection 1). The landlord may object to such a sublease when the property comprises fewer than 13 residential dwellings, when the total number of persons in the dwelling would exceed the number of living rooms, or when the landlord otherwise has reasonable grounds to object to the sublease (§ 158, subsection 2).

Subleases must be concluded in writing, and before the sublease period begins the tenant must send the landlord a copy of the sublease agreement (§ 157, subsection 3).

The landlord must keep the property and the leased premises in proper repair, including maintaining all installations for drainage and for the supply of electricity, gas, water, heating and cooling (§ 112, subsection 2). The landlord is responsible for both the interior and the exterior maintenance (indvendig og udvendig vedligeholdelse) and sets aside amounts for the interior maintenance on a maintenance account for the dwelling (vedligeholdelseskonto) under §§ 116-118. During the tenancy the tenant must maintain and, where necessary, renew locks and keys (§ 112, subsection 3).

The tenant must report damage whose repair cannot be postponed to the landlord immediately, and other damage without undue delay (§ 149, subsection 3). If the landlord does not remedy a defect immediately after a demand, the tenant may remedy it at the landlord’s expense and claim a proportionate rent reduction for as long as the defect reduces the value of the leased premises (§ 95, subsections 1 and 2).

The landlord has access to the leased premises when circumstances require it (§ 102). The landlord may start works in the leased premises with 6 weeks’ notice when carrying them out does not cause the tenant significant inconvenience; before other works the tenant is entitled to 3 months’ notice; urgent repairs may always be carried out without notice (§ 103, subsections 1-3).

Before this lease was concluded, the landlord handed the energy label (energimærkning) for the leased premises to the tenant, as the landlord is obliged to do under § 7, subsection 1, of the Act on the Promotion of Energy Savings in Buildings (lov om fremme af energibesparelser i bygninger), unless the leased premises are exempt from the energy-labelling requirement under the rules laid down under that Act.

The landlord makes the leased premises available to the tenant in good and proper condition; the leased premises must be clean, the windowpanes intact and all external doors fitted with working locks with the corresponding keys (§ 89). If the landlord lets more than 1 residential dwelling, the landlord must inspect the dwelling on moving in, summon the tenant to the inspection and draw up a move-in report (indflytningsrapport), which is handed to the tenant at the inspection; if the tenant is not present at the inspection or will not acknowledge receipt of the report, it is sent to the tenant no later than 2 weeks after the inspection (§ 90, subsections 1 and 2).

If the leased premises are defective at the start of the tenancy, the tenant must notify the landlord no later than 2 weeks after the start of the tenancy that the tenant intends to invoke the defect, unless the defect cannot be detected with ordinary care or the landlord has acted fraudulently (§ 91).

On moving out, the leased premises must be vacated no later than 12.00 noon on the moving-out day and returned in the same condition as at handover, except for deterioration due to wear and tear that is not covered by the tenant’s maintenance obligation and defects that the landlord is obliged to remedy (§ 187, subsection 1). If the landlord lets more than 1 residential dwelling, the landlord must inspect the dwelling no later than 2 weeks after becoming aware of the move-out, summon the tenant in writing with at least 1 week’s notice, unless a shorter notice is agreed when the tenancy has been terminated or rescinded, and draw up a move-out report (fraflytningsrapport); otherwise the landlord’s claim for restoration lapses (§ 187, subsections 3-5). Claims for restoration must be made no later than 2 weeks after the move-out inspection or, for tenancies without a duty to hold a move-out inspection, after the moving-out day, and costs not included in the move-out report are of no concern to the tenant, unless the defect cannot be detected with ordinary care or the tenant has acted fraudulently (§ 187, subsection 2). No later than 8 days before moving out, the tenant gives the address to which notices, including claims for restoration, can be sent (§ 186, subsection 2). The deposit is then settled (see article ).

Unless otherwise agreed, the tenant may terminate the lease with 3 months’ notice to the first weekday of a calendar month that is not the day before a public holiday (§ 169, subsection 1, § 175, subsection 1). However, the tenant may not terminate a lease of the family home without the consent of the tenant’s spouse (§ 169, subsection 2).

The landlord may terminate the lease only in the cases listed in §§ 170 and 171, and subject to the limitations in § 172. The landlord’s notice under § 170, no. 2 and 3, and § 171 must be in writing, state the ground for termination where notice is given under § 171, and inform the tenant of the right to object no later than 6 weeks after the notice has arrived; otherwise it is invalid (§ 176). For notice under § 170, no. 2 and 3, and § 171, subsection 1, no. 1, the tenant is entitled to a notice period of at least 1 year to the first weekday of a calendar month that is not the day before a public holiday (§ 175, subsection 2).

If the tenancy is for a fixed term (see article ), it cannot be terminated by notice during the term, unless this has been agreed or the other party is in breach of the agreement.

The landlord may rescind the lease (hæve lejeaftalen) in the cases listed in § 182, subsection 1, unless the matter is of minor importance (§ 183). For late payment the landlord may rescind the lease only if the tenant has not paid the arrears no later than 14 days after a written payment demand (påkrav) has arrived; the demand may be sent at the earliest after the 3rd weekday after the last day for timely payment and must state expressly that the tenancy may be rescinded if the arrears are not paid before the time limit expires (§ 182, subsection 2). As a fee for the demand the landlord may charge the amount that follows from § 182, subsection 2, and § 204, subsection 1 (335 kr. in 2026 and 344 kr. from 1 January 2027). The landlord’s notice of termination and payment demand cannot be given as digital documents (§ 13, subsection 2). Moving out is governed by what this agreement provides (see article ).

The landlord processes the tenant’s personal data only insofar as this is necessary for concluding and performing this agreement and for complying with legal obligations, in accordance with the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR, Regulation (EU) 2016/679) and the Danish Data Protection Act (databeskyttelsesloven). The data are kept no longer than necessary for those purposes.

The tenant may exercise the rights under the GDPR against the landlord, in particular the rights of access, rectification and erasure, and may lodge a complaint with the Danish Data Protection Agency (Datatilsynet).

This agreement is governed by Danish law.

Disputes that fall within the competence of the rent tribunal under the Rent Act are brought before the rent tribunal (huslejenævnet) of the municipality in which the property is located. Other disputes and appeals against decisions of the rent tribunal are heard by the housing court (boligretten) under the rules of the Housing Conditions Act (lov om boligforhold). If a provision of this agreement is invalid, the validity of the remaining provisions is unaffected; the rules of the Act apply instead.
