Any statutory entitlement of the tenant to a new tenancy at the end of the term is governed by what this lease provides (see article ).

Under Part II of the 1980 Act a tenant may become entitled to a new tenancy of a tenement (business equity) where, at the end of the tenancy, the tenement was, during the whole of the period of 5 years ending at that time, continuously in the occupation of the tenant or the tenant’s predecessors in title and bona fide used wholly or partly for the purpose of carrying on a business (section 13(1)(a) of the 1980 Act); a temporary break in that use may be disregarded if the court considers it reasonable. Section 13(1)(b) and (c) provide further grounds: continuous occupation by the tenant or the tenant’s predecessors in title for the whole of the period of 20 years, and improvements for which the tenant would otherwise be entitled to compensation under Part IV of the 1980 Act and to which not less than 50 per cent of the letting value of the tenement is attributable. Section 17 of the 1980 Act excludes the entitlement in certain cases, among them termination for non-payment of rent or for breach of covenant, termination by the tenant by notice of surrender or otherwise, and the cases of redevelopment with planning permission and good estate management listed in section 17(2).

A claim for a new tenancy requires a notice of intention to claim relief in the prescribed form, served within the time limited by section 20 of the 1980 Act, for a tenancy terminating by the expiration of a term either before its termination or before the expiration of 3 months after the landlord has served, not earlier than 3 months before the termination, notice in the prescribed form of the expiration of the term. Where the court fixes the terms of a new tenancy based on business equity, its duration is 20 years or such less term as the tenant nominates, but not less than 5 years without the landlord’s agreement (section 23(2)). A tenant who is refused a new tenancy may be entitled to compensation for disturbance or for improvements under the 1980 Act.

Any provision of a contract that excludes or restricts the application of the 1980 Act is void, except a renunciation under section 17(1)(a)(iiia) (section 85 of the 1980 Act). Where section 13(1)(a) applies, the tenant is not entitled to a new tenancy if the tenant has renounced in writing, whether for or without valuable consideration, the entitlement to a new tenancy in the tenement and has received independent legal advice in relation to the renunciation (section 17(1)(a)(iiia)).
