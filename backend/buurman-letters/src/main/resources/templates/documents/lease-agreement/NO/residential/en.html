the following agreement on the lease of a dwelling (avtale om leie av bolig) is concluded. If there are several tenants, they are jointly and severally liable (solidarisk) for all obligations under this agreement.

The landlord lets to the tenant the dwelling at address, with the rooms, the appurtenances and the share of common areas specified in this agreement, hereinafter “the premises” (husrommet). The premises are to be used as a dwelling (bolig).

The tenancy is governed by the Act of 26 March 1999 No. 17 relating to tenancy agreements (the Tenancy Act, husleieloven). Terms that are less favourable to the tenant than what follows from the Tenancy Act cannot be agreed or enforced (§ 1-2, paragraph 1). A provision of this agreement that is less favourable to the tenant than the Act therefore cannot be enforced against the tenant.

The tenancy starts on start date. The landlord must place the premises with appurtenances at the tenant’s disposal from that day (§ 2-1, paragraph 1).

The lease is for an indefinite term (tidsubestemt) and runs until it is terminated by notice under the rules of this agreement and of the Tenancy Act (§ 9-1).

The lease is for a fixed term (tidsbestemt) and ends without notice on end date. A fixed-term lease ends without notice when the agreed term expires. The landlord states that the lease cannot be terminated by notice during the agreed term, unless the parties have agreed below that it may be terminated (§ 9-2, paragraph 1).

If the tenancy continues for more than 3 months after the agreed term has expired without the landlord having asked the tenant in writing to move out, the lease has become a lease for an indefinite term (§ 9-2, paragraph 3).

The rent (leie) is amount per month. In addition, the tenant pays separately for consumption as specified in this agreement (see article ).

The rent is set at a fixed amount (§ 3-1, paragraph 1). A rent that is unreasonable in relation to what is usually obtained, at the time of the agreement, on a new letting of similar premises on similar terms cannot be agreed (§ 4-1). A person who has paid a higher rent than is lawful may claim repayment of the difference, unless the payer must be regarded as substantially jointly responsible for the breach (§ 4-4, paragraph 1).

It cannot be agreed that the tenant is to pay other or larger sums of money than those provided for in § 3-1 and § 3-4 to § 3-6 (§ 3-7, paragraph 1).

Changes to the rent are governed by what this agreement provides (see article ).

Either party may require the rent to be changed, without terminating the tenancy, in line with the consumer price index (konsumprisindeksen) of Statistics Norway (Statistisk sentralbyrå), subject to the following limits: the change must not exceed the change in the consumer price index in the period since the rent was last set, the change may take effect at the earliest 1 year after the last setting of the rent took effect, and the other party must be given written notice of at least 1 month before the change can take effect (§ 4-2).

If the tenancy has lasted for at least 2 years and 6 months without any change in the rent other than one that may be required under § 4-2, either party may, without terminating the tenancy, require the rent to be set at the prevailing rent (gjengs leie) at the time of implementation for the letting of similar premises on similar terms; a deduction is made for the part of the rental value that results from the tenant’s improvements and efforts (§ 4-3, paragraph 1). The adjustment may take effect at the earliest 6 months after a written demand for it has been made, and at the earliest 1 year after the previous change in the rent took effect (§ 4-3, paragraph 2). If the parties cannot agree on the prevailing rent, either of them may require it to be set by a valuation board (takstnemnd) (§ 4-3, paragraph 3, § 12-2).

The landlord may give instructions on the method of transfer if this does not entail additional costs or significant inconvenience for the tenant; the tenant nevertheless always has the right to pay through a financial institution entitled to provide such a service in Norway, and is not obliged to transfer rent abroad (§ 3-3, paragraph 1). If the rent falls due on a Saturday or Sunday, a public holiday or on 1 or 17 May, the due date is postponed to the next following working day (§ 3-2, paragraph 2).

In the event of late payment the tenant owes default interest under the Act of 17 December 1976 No. 100 relating to interest on overdue payments etc. (forsinkelsesrenteloven). Other fees or surcharges cannot be agreed (§ 3-7, paragraph 1).

The premises may only be used as a dwelling for the tenant and the tenant’s household. The tenant may not use the premises for any purpose other than the one agreed and must treat them with due care (§ 5-1, paragraphs 2 and 3).

The tenant must follow the usual house rules and reasonable instructions that the landlord has laid down to ensure good order. Even if the landlord has prohibited keeping animals in the property, the tenant may keep animals if there are good reasons for it and the keeping of animals is not a nuisance to the landlord or the other users of the property (§ 5-2, paragraph 2). Unless otherwise agreed, the landlord must maintain ordinary peace and order in the property (§ 5-2, paragraph 1).

The tenant has the right to take into the household the tenant’s spouse or cohabitant, the tenant’s own relatives or those of the spouse or cohabitant in the direct ascending or descending line, and foster children. Taking other persons into the household requires the landlord’s approval, which may be refused only if that person’s circumstances give an objective reason for it or the premises clearly become overcrowded (§ 7-1).

The tenant may not sublet (framleie) or otherwise transfer the right of use to others without the landlord’s consent, unless otherwise provided by agreement or statute (§ 7-2).

A tenant who is to live in the dwelling may sublet part of the dwelling with the landlord’s approval (§ 7-3). The tenant may sublet the dwelling with the landlord’s approval for up to 2 years during a temporary absence because of work, education, military service, illness or other compelling reasons (§ 7-4). In these cases approval may be refused only if the subtenant’s circumstances give an objective reason for it or the premises clearly become overcrowded.

If the lease is for a fixed term, the tenant may sublet for the rest of the term with the landlord’s approval; if approval is refused although the subtenant’s circumstances give no objective reason for it and the premises do not clearly become overcrowded, the tenant may terminate with the notice referred to in § 9-6 (§ 7-5). If the landlord does not answer a written application for approval within 1 month of receiving it, this counts as approval (§ 7-6).

Unless otherwise agreed, the landlord must keep the premises and the rest of the property during the tenancy in the condition to which the tenant is entitled under Chapter 2 of the Tenancy Act (§ 5-3, paragraph 1).

Unless otherwise agreed, the tenant must maintain door locks, taps, toilets, electrical sockets and switches, water heaters and fixtures and equipment in the premises that are not part of the real property, and must carry out the necessary function checks, cleaning, battery replacement, testing and the like of smoke alarms and fire extinguishing equipment. If items belonging to the landlord must be replaced, this is the landlord’s responsibility unless otherwise agreed. Repair of accidental damage does not count as such maintenance (§ 5-3, paragraphs 2 and 3).

If the tenant discovers damage to the premises that must be repaired without delay, the tenant must notify the landlord immediately; other damage must be reported within a reasonable time (§ 5-5, paragraph 1). The tenant may not make alterations to the premises or to the rest of the property without the landlord’s consent (§ 5-4, paragraph 2). If the landlord does not fulfil the landlord’s obligations, the tenant has the rights set out in § 5-7.

The tenant must, to the necessary extent, give the landlord access to the premises for inspection and to carry out required maintenance and other work as referred to in § 5-6. The tenant must be notified within a reasonable time before an inspection or maintenance work is carried out, unless measures are necessary to prevent damage and it is not possible to notify the tenant (§ 5-6, paragraph 3). Before alterations as referred to in § 5-4 are started, the tenant must be notified with the period referred to in § 9-6, paragraph 1.

Unless otherwise agreed, the premises with appurtenances must on handover be cleared, cleaned and in ordinary good condition (§ 2-2, paragraph 1). Unless otherwise agreed, handover is deemed to have taken place when the tenant has received the keys and otherwise unhindered access to the premises (§ 2-1, paragraph 2). The parties inspect the premises together at the takeover and record the condition, any defects, the meter readings and the keys handed over in a handover report (overtakelsesprotokoll) signed by both parties.

The tenant loses the right to rely on a defect if the landlord has not been notified that the defect is invoked within a reasonable time after the tenant ought to have discovered it, unless the landlord has acted with gross negligence or contrary to honesty and good faith (§ 2-8).

On the day the tenancy ends, the tenant must place the premises with appurtenances at the landlord’s disposal. Unless otherwise agreed, the premises must be cleared, cleaned and in the same condition as at the takeover, except for deterioration due to ordinary wear and tear and defects that the landlord is obliged to remedy (§ 10-2, paragraphs 1 and 2). In the period before moving out, the tenant must, to a reasonable extent, give prospective tenants access to view the premises (§ 10-1).

If the premises are in a worse condition than agreed or laid down in § 10-2, the landlord may claim the necessary costs of repair; the claim must be made within a reasonable time after the landlord ought to have discovered the defect, unless the tenant has acted with gross negligence or contrary to honesty and good faith (§ 10-3, paragraph 2). The deposit is then paid out under the rules of this agreement (see article ).

The tenant may terminate a lease for an indefinite term by notice (oppsigelse) (§ 9-4). The notice period is 3 months, expiring at the end of a calendar month (§ 9-6, paragraph 1).

The landlord may terminate a lease of a dwelling for an indefinite term only if the premises are to be used as a dwelling by the landlord or a member of the landlord’s household, demolition or rebuilding of the property requires the premises to be vacated, the tenant has breached the lease, or there is another objective reason (saklig grunn) to terminate the lease (§ 9-5, paragraph 2). The notice period is the same (§ 9-6, paragraph 1).

A notice of termination from the landlord must be in writing and state the reasons. It must state that the tenant may object (protestere) in writing to the landlord within 1 month after receiving the notice, and that if the tenant does not object within that period, the tenant loses the right to claim that the notice is contrary to the Act, and that the landlord may then request enforced eviction (tvangsfravikelse) under § 13-2, paragraph 3, letter c, of the Enforcement Act (tvangsfullbyrdelsesloven) (§ 9-7, paragraphs 1 and 2). A notice that does not meet these requirements is invalid; if the tenant has moved out in accordance with the notice, it is deemed to have been accepted (§ 9-7, paragraph 3).

If the tenant has objected in time, the notice lapses unless the landlord has brought legal proceedings against the tenant within 3 months after the tenant’s deadline expired (§ 9-8, paragraph 1). The court must set the notice aside if, on an assessment of both parties’ circumstances, it finds that the notice would be unreasonable, but not if the notice is due to a material breach on the part of the tenant (§ 9-8, paragraph 2).

If the lease is for a fixed term (see article ), it cannot be terminated by notice during the agreed term, unless it has been agreed that it may be terminated, or the landlord has failed in the duty to state in writing that it cannot be terminated (§ 9-2, paragraph 2).

The landlord may rescind (heve) the lease because of a material breach on the part of the tenant in the cases listed in § 9-9, paragraph 1, among others where the tenant materially fails to fulfil the obligation to pay rent. The declaration of rescission must be in writing and state the reason for the rescission (§ 9-9, paragraph 2). Where there are grounds for requesting eviction, the landlord may, if there is reason to do so, send written notice of the breach to the social services of the municipality (sosialtjenesten), with a copy to the tenant, unless the tenant has opted out of this in writing (§ 9-11).

The tenant accepts that enforced eviction (tvangsfravikelse) may be required when the rent is not paid (§ 13-2, paragraph 3, letter a, of the Enforcement Act). The tenant also accepts that enforced eviction may be required when the term has expired (§ 13-2, paragraph 3, letter b, of the Enforcement Act).

Enforcement may be requested when 2 weeks have passed after the landlord sent written notice (varsel) to the tenant; the notice may be sent at the earliest on the due date and must draw attention to the fact that enforcement will be requested if the claim is not met (§ 4-18, paragraph 1, of the Enforcement Act). Notice may be dispensed with only in the cases referred to in § 4-18, paragraph 2, of the Enforcement Act. In the case of non-payment, the notice must state that enforcement can be avoided if the rent, out-of-court collection costs, legal costs and rent due up to the time of payment, with interest, are paid before enforcement is carried out (§ 13-2, paragraph 3, letter a, of the Enforcement Act).

This agreement is governed by Norwegian law.

Disputes concerning the lease of a dwelling must be dealt with by the Rent Disputes Tribunal (Husleietvistutvalget) before they can be brought before the district court (tingretten), where the rules of civil procedure would otherwise have required a hearing before the conciliation board (forliksrådet); the conciliation board cannot hear disputes concerning the lease of a dwelling (§ 12-5, paragraph 4). An agreement that disputes are to be decided by arbitration is binding only if it was concluded after the dispute arose (§ 12-1). If the parties cannot agree on the market rent, the prevailing rent or compensation for improvements, either of them may require the dispute to be decided by a valuation board (takstnemnd) (§ 12-2, paragraph 1).

If a provision of this agreement is invalid, this does not affect the validity of the other provisions of the agreement; the rules of the Tenancy Act apply instead.
