The landlord’s notice of termination must be given in writing and must state the date on which the tenancy ends and the ground for the termination (LHVL section 43, subsection 1). If a tenancy for an indefinite period ends on a notice of termination by the landlord that cannot be regarded as consistent with good practice in tenancies, the tenant is entitled to compensation for repair and alteration works that have increased the value of the premises and for moving costs and the costs of acquiring new premises (LHVL section 45, subsection 2). A tenant who, on the basis of the lease, has carried on trade, craft, industrial business or other gainful activity in the premises for at least 2 years is in addition entitled to reasonable compensation for the reduction or loss of clientele caused by the move and for the increase in the rental value of the premises resulting from the tenant’s activity, if the next occupant of the premises starts to carry on substantially similar gainful activity in them (LHVL section 45, subsection 2). A term restricting these rights is void (LHVL section 47). The tenant loses these rights if, after the notice of termination, the tenant neglects its obligations as tenant other than to a minor extent (LHVL section 46).
