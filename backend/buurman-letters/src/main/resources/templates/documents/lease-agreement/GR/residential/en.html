Notice of termination of a lease of indefinite term, where the rent is set by the month, is given, unless otherwise agreed, at least 15 days in advance and takes effect at the end of the calendar month (article 609 of the Civil Code); the lease, however, lasts at least 3 years, unless that term has been lawfully shortened.

If the tenant is in arrears with the rent, wholly or in part, the landlord is entitled to terminate the lease by notice given at least 1 month in advance in the case of a lease whose term was agreed for 1 year or more, and 10 days in advance in other leases, without prejudice to the landlord’s claim for damages for the early ending of the lease; the notice has no effect if the tenant, before that period expires, pays the rent in arrears together with any costs of the notice (article 597 of the Civil Code). Any agreement that shortens these periods, or ends the lease automatically, or gives the landlord such a right as soon as the tenant is in default with the payment of the rent, is void (article 598 of the Civil Code).

The tenant is entitled to terminate the lease, in particular: if the agreed use was not granted to the tenant unhindered in good time, wholly or in part, or was later withdrawn, after setting the landlord a reasonable period to restore it which has expired without result, or even without setting a period if, because of that ground, the tenant has no interest in the performance of the agreement, save in the cases for which the landlord is not liable (articles 585 and 586 of the Civil Code); without setting a period, if use of the leased premises entails a serious risk to the health of the tenant or of the tenant’s relatives living with the tenant, even if the tenant knew of the dangerous conditions or waived the relevant rights (article 588 of the Civil Code); and, where the tenant is a civil servant transferred to another place, from the transfer, under the rules on leases of indefinite term (article 613 of the Civil Code).

On the tenant’s death, the tenant’s heirs are entitled to terminate the lease by notice given at least 3 months in advance, taking effect at the end of a calendar month; if the leased premises served as the family home within the meaning of article 1393 of the Civil Code and the tenant’s spouse is alive, the rights and obligations under the lease pass exclusively to the spouse, who may, observing the same period, terminate the lease at any time (article 612 of the Civil Code). If the leased premises serve as the family home and that use has been notified to the landlord, a notice of termination by the landlord is void unless it is also served on the tenant’s spouse, observing the same period, if any, required for the notice (article 612A of the Civil Code).

The landlord may apply for an order for possession (διαταγή απόδοσης της χρήσης μισθίου) in the event of arrears of rent due to obstinacy (δυστροπία) or of the expiry of the fixed term of the lease, under the conditions of article 637 of the Code of Civil Procedure (Κώδικας Πολιτικής Δικονομίας, ΚΠολΔ): the start or the end of the lease must be proved in writing and a written demand must have been served by a judicial officer at least 15 days before the application is filed, which is required only the first time; payment of the rent within that period, proved in writing, prevents the order from being issued, unless there are repeated arrears due to obstinacy. For an order on the ground of the expiry of the term of the lease, the landlord must in addition serve an extrajudicial request to return the leased premises at least 3 months before that term expires or, for a lease that has become one of indefinite term after its expiry, at least 3 months before the order is served; in that case enforcement may begin once 2 months have passed since the order was served on the tenant.
