The parties may end the lease at any time by written agreement. Notice of termination (výpověď) is given in writing and must be delivered to the other party.

The tenant may terminate the fixed-term lease before the agreed term expires if (a) the tenant loses the capacity to carry on the activity for which the premises are intended; (b) the premises cease, for objective reasons, to be fit for the activity for which they were intended and the landlord does not provide the tenant with suitable substitute premises; or (c) the landlord grossly breaches its obligations towards the tenant (section 2308 of the Civil Code).

The landlord may terminate the fixed-term lease before the agreed term expires if (a) the property in which the premises are located is to be removed, or rebuilt in a way that prevents the further use of the premises, and the landlord neither had to nor could foresee this when the agreement was concluded; or (b) the tenant grossly breaches its obligations towards the landlord, in particular by acting contrary to section 2305 of the Civil Code although the landlord has called on the tenant to remedy this, or by being in delay with the payment of the rent or of the services connected with the use of the premises for more than 1 month (section 2309 of the Civil Code).

The notice must state its ground; a notice that does not state its ground is invalid (section 2310(1) of the Civil Code). The notice period (výpovědní doba) is 3 months (section 2310(2) of the Civil Code). Other grounds of notice during the fixed term apply only if they are agreed here together with the notice period (section 2229 of the Civil Code): ______________________________

Either party may terminate the lease for an indefinite term with a notice period of 6 months; where the party has a serious reason for the notice, the notice period is 3 months; where the lease has lasted for more than 5 years and, in view of the circumstances, the party could not have expected the other party to give notice, the notice period is always 6 months (section 2312 of the Civil Code).

The party given notice may raise written objections to it within 1 month of the day the notice was delivered; if it does not do so in time, the right to have the justification of the notice reviewed lapses. If the objections were raised in time and the party giving notice does not withdraw the notice within 1 month of their delivery, the party given notice may ask the court to review the justification of the notice within 2 months of the expiry of that period (section 2314 of the Civil Code). If the tenant vacates the premises in accordance with the notice, the notice is deemed valid and accepted by the tenant without objections (section 2313 of the Civil Code).

If a party breaches its obligations in a particularly serious manner and thereby causes considerable harm to the other party, the other party may terminate the lease without a notice period (section 2232 of the Civil Code). The landlord may also terminate the lease without a notice period where the tenant, despite a written warning with a reasonable time for remedy, uses the premises so that they wear out beyond what is reasonable or are at risk of destruction, or does not pay the rent even by the due date of the next rent (section 2228 of the Civil Code), and in the other cases provided by law.

If the owner of the premises changes, the rights and obligations under the lease pass to the new owner (section 2221(1) of the Civil Code). A party may not terminate the lease merely because the owner has changed, save in the cases and within the time limits of section 2222 of the Civil Code.
