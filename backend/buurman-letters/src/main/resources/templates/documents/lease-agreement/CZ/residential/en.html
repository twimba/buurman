The agreement requires written form; the landlord may not plead against the tenant that the agreement is invalid for lack of form (section 2237 of the Civil Code).

If the parties have not agreed increases of the rent and have not expressly excluded them, the landlord may propose to the tenant in writing an increase of the rent up to the comparable rent usual in the given place (srovnatelné nájemné obvyklé v daném místě), provided that the proposed increase, together with the increases made in the last 3 years, does not exceed 20 %. The comparable rent usual in the given place is established by an expert opinion or on the basis of evidence of at least 3 comparable rents (section 3 of Government Regulation No. 453/2013 Coll.).

If the tenant agrees to the proposal, the tenant pays the increased rent from the 3rd calendar month after the proposal was received. If the tenant does not inform the landlord in writing within 2 months of receiving the proposal that the tenant agrees, the landlord may, within a further 3 months, ask the court to determine the rent; the court decides on a rent up to the amount usual in the place and at the time, with effect from the day the application was filed (section 2249(3) of the Civil Code). Failing agreement, the landlord may propose an increase on these grounds of 3.5 % of the costs incurred per year (section 2250 of the Civil Code).

If the tenant dies and the lease is not a joint lease, the lease passes to a member of the tenant’s household who lived in the flat on the day of the tenant’s death and has no flat of their own; if that person is not the tenant’s spouse, partner, parent, sibling, son-in-law, daughter-in-law, child or grandchild, the lease passes to that person only if the landlord has consented (section 2279(1) of the Civil Code).

A lease that has passed in this way ends at the latest 2 years after the day it passed; this does not apply where the person concerned had reached the age of 70 years on the day the lease passed; where that person had not reached the age of 18 years, the lease ends at the latest on the day that person reaches the age of 20 years, unless the landlord and the tenant agree otherwise (section 2279(2) of the Civil Code). Each person who meets the conditions may notify the landlord in writing within 1 month of the tenant’s death that they do not intend to continue the lease (section 2279(4) of the Civil Code).

If the lease does not pass to a member of the household, it passes to the tenant’s heir (section 2282 of the Civil Code); the landlord and the heir may then terminate the lease with a notice period of 2 months within the time limits of section 2283 of the Civil Code.

The parties may end the lease at any time by written agreement.

Notice of termination (výpověď) requires written form and must reach the other party. The notice period (výpovědní doba) runs from the first day of the calendar month following the month in which the notice reached the other party (section 2286(1) of the Civil Code).

The tenant may terminate a lease for an indefinite term by notice without giving reasons; the notice period is 3 months (section 2231 of the Civil Code). The tenant may terminate a lease for a fixed term if the circumstances on which the parties evidently relied when the lease was concluded change to such an extent that the tenant cannot reasonably be required to continue the lease (section 2287 of the Civil Code). The tenant may terminate the lease without a notice period where section 2266 of the Civil Code so provides and in the other cases provided by law.

The landlord may terminate the lease only on the grounds provided by law. A lease for a fixed or indefinite term may be terminated by the landlord with a notice period of 3 months if (a) the tenant grossly breaches a duty arising from the lease; (b) the tenant is convicted of an intentional criminal offence committed against the landlord or a member of the landlord’s household or a person living in the building where the flat is located, or against property of others located in that building; (c) the flat must be vacated because, in the public interest, the flat or the building must be dealt with in such a way that the flat cannot be used at all; or (d) there is another similarly serious ground (section 2288(1) of the Civil Code).

A lease for an indefinite term may also be terminated by the landlord with a notice period of 3 months if (a) the flat is to be used by the landlord, or by the landlord’s spouse who intends to leave the family household where a petition for divorce has been filed or the marriage has already been dissolved by divorce; or (b) the landlord needs the flat for a relative of the landlord or of the landlord’s spouse in the direct line or in the collateral line in the 2nd degree (section 2288(2) of the Civil Code). If the landlord does not use the flat for the stated purpose within 1 month of its vacation by the tenant, the landlord must let the flat to the tenant again or compensate the tenant for the damage, subject to the conditions of section 2289 of the Civil Code.

The landlord states the ground in the notice (section 2288(3) of the Civil Code) and informs the tenant of the tenant’s right to raise objections to the notice and to ask the court to review whether the notice is justified; otherwise the notice is invalid (section 2286(2) of the Civil Code). The tenant may apply to the court for that review within 2 months of the day the notice reached the tenant (section 2290 of the Civil Code).

If the tenant breaches a duty in a particularly serious manner, the landlord may terminate the lease without a notice period and require the tenant to hand the flat back without undue delay, at the latest within 1 month of the end of the lease (section 2291(1) of the Civil Code). Such a breach is, in particular, failing to pay the rent and the costs of services for at least 3 months, damaging the flat or the building in a serious or irreparable manner, otherwise causing serious damage or difficulties to the landlord or to persons living in the building, or using the flat without authorisation in another way or for another purpose than agreed (section 2291(2) of the Civil Code). The notice is disregarded if it does not state what the landlord considers to be the particularly serious breach, or if the landlord did not, before delivering the notice, call on the tenant to remedy the conduct or the unlawful situation within a reasonable time (section 2291(3) of the Civil Code).

The landlord may not terminate the lease of a flat in which the tenant lives on the ground of a change of ownership (section 2224 of the Civil Code); the rights and obligations under the lease pass to the new owner (section 2221(1) of the Civil Code).
